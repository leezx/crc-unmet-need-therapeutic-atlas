## What would close Module B's actual malignant-cell prevalence question for this target

**Attempted 2026-08-23** via `scripts/infercnv_lite_gse178318.py` — a chromosome-arm-level CNV score (gene-position reference from `DATA/1.Databases/HGNC_gene_id_mapping`, resolved via `config/external_sources.yaml`'s `module_b_gene_position_reference` `path_env_var` and checksum-locked via `DATA/reference/hgnc_gene_id_mapping_source_lock.tsv`) against a held-out half of a tumor-site-immune reference population, both restricted to the 3 treated patients (`COL15`/`COL17`/`COL18`) matching `TE004`'s treated-cohort `indication_id`. **Result: ambiguous, not confirmatory** — see `analysis_contracts/infercnv_lite_gse178318.md` for the full method and honest results. Epithelial-proxy cells (n=1,786) show a modest median CNV-score shift over the held-out treated-cohort reference (n=19,002; 1.32 vs 0.63, directionally consistent with malignancy) but the two distributions overlap substantially, and only 36/1,786 (2.02%) of cells individually clear the held-out reference's own 99th-percentile threshold, vs. 1.00% for the reference's own cells at that same threshold (enrichment ratio 2.02x) — too few per sample for a reliable confirmed-malignant CEACAM5 prevalence number. This coarse arm-level method is missing within-cell gene-order smoothing and a centering/reference-subtraction step relative to real InferCNV workflows (not primarily "no cross-cell smoothing"), and lineage-driven expression differences (epithelial vs immune, unrelated to copy number) are an equally plausible, co-equal alternative explanation for the shift — neither is evidence that the epithelial-proxy population isn't malignant.

**Attempted again, 2026-08-26 (Next-handoff item 1, revised in round 1 and round 2 review of PR #89), via `scripts/infercnv_gse178318.py`** — real gene-window InferCNV using `infercnvpy` (a maintained, `inferCNV`-inspired Python implementation, explicitly experimental per its own documentation, not a validated equivalent of Broad `inferCNV` — corrected from an earlier "peer-established"/"field-standard" overstatement), addressing the two gaps the arm-level attempt's own limitations named: real gene-order window smoothing (via a new base-pair gene-position table, `DATA/reference/ensembl_gene_positions_grch38_release110.tsv`, 97.4% gene coverage) and explicit reference-centering (`infercnvpy`'s own `reference=` parameter). Same populations as the arm-level attempt, confirmed identical by **direct cell-set comparison** (not just matching counts — round 1 review required this): 38,003 reference / 1,786 epithelial-proxy cells, exact set match — the only variable changed is the algorithm. Round 1 review also found and fixed a real bug (mitochondrial genes mapped to `"chrMT"`, which `infercnvpy`'s own windowing code does not recognize as excludable — only the literal `"chrM"` is — so mitochondrial RNA signal silently contaminated the CNV score); the entire analysis was re-run after the fix. **Result: materially sharper, still not DNA-level.** `CNV_HIGH` enrichment among epithelial-proxy cells: 391/1,786 (21.89%) vs. the held-out reference's own 190/19,002 (1.00%) — descriptive tail-enrichment ratio **21.89x** (versus the arm-level attempt's 2.02x; the pre-fix number was a close-but-confirmed-different 22.79x/407 cells). `CEACAM5`-positive fraction is higher in `CNV_HIGH` than `CNV_LOW` cells in every one of the 6 sample strata (COL15 CRC 0.7353 vs 0.5435; LM 0.6456 vs 0.2315; COL17 CRC 0.6000 vs 0.1556; LM 1.0000 vs 0.0833; COL18 CRC 0.7727 vs 0.2727; LM 0.7455 vs 0.1377). Two new sanity checks were added (at the reviewer's request): a leave-`CEACAM5`-out control cleanly rules out material `CEACAM5`-expression leakage as a driver of `CNV_HIGH` membership (<1% change in the `CNV_HIGH` cell set, Jaccard=0.992) — this rules out one specific artifact, not the separate, still-open question of whether `CNV_HIGH` itself represents malignant cells. The block-coherence check, corrected in round 2 review to test actual spatial contiguity and sign (not just magnitude concentration), gives a genuinely mixed, honestly-disclosed result: `CNV_HIGH`'s `chr7`/`chr20` signal (independently confirmed recurrent CRC copy-number-gain hotspots) is real, spatially contiguous, and consistently gain-like — but the identical broad pattern also appears, at smaller magnitude, in `CNV_LOW` and even reference cells (for `chr20`, the exact same window range in all three groups) — a graded-magnitude finding on a shared spatial pattern, not one exclusive to `CNV_HIGH`. Still expression-based CNV inference, not a DNA-level call — run with `infercnvpy`'s own unturned package defaults. Since `CNV_HIGH`/`CNV_LOW` are themselves defined by splitting on CNV magnitude, this graded pattern is not independent confirmation of malignant-cell identity, and is recorded at `confidence=LOW` (revised down from an earlier round's `MEDIUM` in round 3 review of PR #89) rather than this module's usual `MEDIUM`. Full method, every limitation, and results: `analysis_contracts/infercnv_gse178318.md` (`TE043`/`EV051`).
